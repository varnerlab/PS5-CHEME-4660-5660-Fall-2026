Problem Set 5: Does a Covered Call Fund Beat Holding Its Shares?

Release: Sunday, November 8, 2026
Initial submission deadline: Sunday, November 22, 2026, at 11:59 PM ET
Maximum score: 4 points

A covered call fund owns shares and a cash account, and every month sells one call contract for every 100 shares it owns. The premiums go into the fund's cash. If the share price ends above the strike, the fund gives up every gain above the strike. In PS5 you run such a fund twice for each of 29 tickers (stocks and exchange-traded funds). First you run the fund in a model, which simulates the share price with geometric Brownian motion and prices the calls on the binomial tree as in the course example. Then you run the fund on the 203 trades in the data, sold from April to October 2026, at the mid prices in the data. You compare the fund's growth rate with the growth rates of holding the shares (the same shares and cash, selling no calls), SPY, and cash, and you compare the model's prediction with what happened. The Advanced track adds a collar fund, which also buys a put each month. You are graded on correct calculations and interpretation, never on whether the fund beats holding the shares.
